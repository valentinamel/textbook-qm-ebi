Tutorial 2: Multiple regression and extensions

By the end of Tutorial 2, you can

  • fit and interpret a multiple regression in R
  • test single coefficients and groups of coefficients against zero
  • interpret dummy, interaction, and quadratic terms correctly
  • compare nested models with a partial F-test
  • read a regression table as printed in a research paper
  • write a complete analysis that is ready for the exam format and states its limits.

Tutorials are exam preparation sessions. Every task below follows the format of the final exam. You first try each task in pairs. You may use AI for this, following Attempt, Ask, Verify, Explain. Then the solution is discussed together.

The tasks follow the order of the course. Task 1 uses Lecture 4, Task 2 uses Lecture 5, and Tasks 3 and 4 use Lecture 6. A regression table then puts the models of Tasks 2 to 4 side by side. Task 5 applies Lectures 3 to 5 to a table in the style of a published paper.

In the tutorial

Try each task in R before you open the answer. Tasks 1 to 4 use the Shark Tank file, and money is measured in thousands of dollars (ask_1k). First build the data for the analysis. The code is the same as in Lectures 4–6:

sharks <- read.csv("data/shark_tank_teaching.csv", stringsAsFactors = FALSE)
sharks$ask_1k <- sharks$ask_amount_usd / 1000
known_gender <- subset(sharks, pitcher_gender_group != "unknown")
known_gender$gender <- relevel(factor(known_gender$pitcher_gender_group),
                               ref = "all_men")
known_gender$ask_1k <- known_gender$ask_amount_usd / 1000
known_gender$women <- as.integer(
  known_gender$pitcher_gender_group %in% c("all_women", "mixed")
)
known_gender$season_c <- known_gender$season - mean(known_gender$season)

Check your answer

The reference category is all-men. At the same season and requested amount, all-women presenting teams are fitted to offer about 1.28 equity percentage points more than all-men teams, and mixed teams about 0.12 points more. The unit is percentage points, not percent. The null hypothesis of the partial F-test is that both dummy coefficients are zero. \(F\approx3.76\) and p \(\approx\) 0.023, so reject at 5%. The two terms are jointly useful, even though the t-test of the mixed dummy alone (p \(\approx\) 0.82) is far from significant. The F-test asks whether the two terms together improve the model. Each t-test asks about one comparison with the reference group. The categories are broad and the data do not come from an experiment, so the result does not show a causal effect of gender.

  2. Calculate both season slopes.

  4. Explain what centering does.
Check your answer

At a fixed requested amount, the fitted line for all-men teams is \(13.625-0.799\,season\_c\) plus the term for the requested amount. For teams with women the intercept is \(13.625+0.807=14.432\) and the slope is \(-0.7994-0.0615=-0.861\). So the season slope is about \(-0.799\) percentage points per season for all-men teams and about \(-0.861\) for teams with women. The estimated difference in slopes is \(-0.061\) percentage points per season with p \(\approx\) 0.520. The model gives no evidence that the season trends differ between the two groups. It also does not prove that the trends are equal, because the estimate is imprecise. Centering makes the women coefficient compare the groups at the average season (about 8.8) and not at Season 0, which does not exist. It changes neither the fitted values nor the interaction estimate.

  1. Why must I(season^2) be used in the R formula?
  2. Decide with the partial F-test whether the quadratic term improves the model.
  3. Calculate the fitted change from Season 10 to Season 11.
  4. State why the curve should not be extrapolated beyond Season 16.
Check your answer

I() tells R to calculate the square. Without it, R would read ^ as formula syntax. The partial F-test gives \(F\approx20.1\) with p \(<\) 0.001, so the quadratic term improves the model. With \(\hat\beta_1\approx-1.713\) and \(\hat\beta_2\approx0.051\), the change from Season 10 to 11 is \(\hat\beta_1+\hat\beta_2(2\times10+1)=\hat\beta_1+21\hat\beta_2\approx-0.64\) percentage points. Never read \(\hat\beta_1\) alone while the squared term is in the model. Beyond Season 16 the fitted curve turns upward, and no data support that turn. A quadratic curve can change direction sharply outside the observed range.

Task 5: Read a regression table from a paper

The outcome is entrepreneurial intention on a scale from 1 to 7, measured in a survey of 265 students. Self-efficacy is a score from 1 to 7 for how confident a student is that he or she can start a business. The intercept is left out, as some journals do.

Outcome: entrepreneurial intention (scale from 1 to 7). Ordinary least squares. Standard errors in parentheses. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

  1. What do the numbers in parentheses and the stars mean? Check the stars of the self-efficacy coefficient with its t statistic.
  2. Interpret the self-efficacy coefficient in column (2) in one complete sentence.
  3. The coefficient of Female changes from \(-0.21\) with a star in column (1) to \(-0.15\) without a star in column (2). Explain what happened with the ideas of Lecture 4, and say what the missing star does and does not mean.
  4. \(R^2\) rises from 0.03 to 0.19. What does that say, and which test would judge whether the two added predictors are jointly useful?
Check your answer
  1. The numbers in parentheses are standard errors. The stars show the p-value of the two-sided t-test of each coefficient against zero. For self-efficacy, \(t=0.38/0.07\approx5.4\). This is far beyond the multiplier \(t_{0.975,\,260}\approx1.97\), and p is below 0.001, so three stars are correct.
  2. Among the 265 surveyed students, a self-efficacy score that is one point higher is associated with an entrepreneurial intention that is 0.38 points higher, holding age, gender, and prior experience fixed.
  3. Model (2) holds two more variables fixed than model (1). The Female coefficient now compares women and men with the same experience and self-efficacy. Part of the raw gap of 0.21 points went together with differences in those two variables. The missing star says that the confidence interval around \(-0.15\) includes zero. It does not show that the gap is zero.
  4. The two added predictors account for a further 16 percentage points of the variation in intentions in this sample. A partial F-test of the two nested models, anova(reduced, complete), would judge whether they are jointly useful.
