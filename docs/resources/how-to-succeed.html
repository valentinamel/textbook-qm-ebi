How to succeed in this course

Success in QM is not about talent for mathematics. It is about a short weekly routine that you repeat: prepare before class, take part in class, read and practise after class, and bring your attempts to the tutorial. Every lecture chapter follows this routine. Every part of it has a stated time budget, so you always know how much work is enough.

Why this routine works:

  • If you prepare, the lecture is your second look at each idea, not your first. You can then use the lecture for questions and examples.
  • The lecture covers the main ideas of each chapter and leaves room for questions and for trying tasks yourself. The chapter has the full material, with every step, example, and piece of code. This is why the first step after class is to read the lecture material once in full.
  • Answering a few recall questions on the same day helps you remember the material. On that day, this costs you the least time.
  • Each chapter has review questions on earlier lectures, because the exam covers the whole course.
  • The tutorials prepare you for the exam only if you arrive with your own attempts. Wrong attempts are the most useful ones.

The total time outside class is about 4 hours per week for QM Basic in weeks with two lectures (about the same in tutorial weeks), and about 1.5 to 2 hours per week for QM Intermediate (about 3.5 hours in the two tutorial weeks). Practice spread over the weeks works better than the same hours packed together before the exam.

Minimum path and full path

Every chapter marks two paths through the work outside class.

  • Minimum path. Before class: the video and the prediction (QM Basic) or the video and the R warm-up (QM Intermediate). After class: read the lecture material, answer the recall questions, then work for 10 minutes on the tasks that follow. This takes about 35 minutes after class. If a week is very busy, do only this. It keeps you up to date with the course.
  • Full path. All tasks in the chapter, with the stated time budgets. The exam expects this level of preparation.

What is examined

Each lecture has a Must know box. It separates what you must be able to do yourself from what it is enough to recognise. Definition boxes belong to the exam material. Grey Optional boxes do not belong to the exam material. They contain formal versions, derivations, and extra topics for students who are interested.

In class the lecturer will often ask you to try a question with your neighbour for two or three minutes before showing the answer. Take these minutes seriously, even when the question looks hard. Most of the learning happens in these minutes. In QM Intermediate, bring your laptop. The code of the lecture is in the chapter, ready to copy. You can run each step yourself and get the same numbers and figures.

Exams. Both courses end with one exam that counts for 100% of the grade. All exam questions are open questions. The QM Basic exam is a closed-book written exam of 180 minutes, with the formula sheet attached. You may use a calculator of the Casio FX-82 series or of the Texas Instruments TI-30 series, but not the TI-30X Pro. You take the QM Intermediate exam in TestVision, and it lasts 120 minutes. You may use RStudio, Excel, the TestVision calculator, a simple pocket calculator, scrap paper, and the code sheet.

Each exam has 100 points. Your test score is the number of points divided by 10, with two decimals and a minimum of 1.00. The lecturer enters this score in Osiris, and Osiris rounds it to a whole or half grade for your final course grade. A score from 5.50 to 5.74 becomes 6.0, and a score from 5.25 to 5.49 becomes 5.0. For example, 72 points give a test score of 7.20 and a final grade of 7.0. You pass with a final grade of 6.0 or higher, that is with 55 points or more.

Printable weekly checklists

The course period has four types of week. Print this page or copy the relevant list into your planner at the start of each week.

QM Basic: week with two lectures (weeks 43, 45, 47)

Two-lecture week, about 4 hours outside class

  • Friday before (30 min, free morning): preparation videos for Monday’s lecture, preparation task, prediction
  • Monday: 14:45 lecture · evening, 25 min: read the lecture material, answer the recall questions
  • Tuesday (60 min, free day): practice tasks for Monday’s lecture · review questions from earlier lectures
  • Wednesday (30 min, around your one EiA session): preparation videos for Thursday’s lecture, preparation task, prediction
  • Thursday: 10:45 lecture · afternoon, 25 min: read the lecture material, answer the recall questions
  • Friday (60 min): practice tasks for Thursday’s lecture · “Make it yours”, two sentences · prepare for Monday

QM Basic: week with one lecture and a tutorial (weeks 44, 46, 48, 49)

Lecture-plus-tutorial week

  • Friday before (30 min): preparation for Monday’s lecture
  • Monday: lecture (14:45, in week 44 at 10:45) · 25 min: read the lecture material, answer the recall questions
  • Tuesday (60 min): practice tasks for Monday’s lecture
  • Wednesday (60 min): attempt the tutorial tasks yourself first (AI allowed, following the protocol below), mark where you got stuck
  • Thursday: tutorial at 10:45 or 12:45 (exam preparation session), bring your attempts · 15 min: redo the task you could not solve
  • Friday (60 min): finish the tutorial set on your own · extra exercises with worked solutions from Tilly · “Make it yours” · prepare for Monday

QM Intermediate: standard week (one Wednesday lecture)

In QM Intermediate you do one round of work per week, spread over several days.

Standard week, 1.5 to 2 hours outside class

  • Monday or Tuesday (15–30 min, before the lecture): preparation video, preparation question, R warm-up, bring the output
  • Wednesday: QM lecture (the start time changes, see the table above), bring your laptop · evening, 25 min: read the lecture material, answer the recall questions
  • Thursday or Friday (50–60 min, around your EiA3 slot): the practice tasks after class · one review question from an earlier lecture · “Make it yours”, two sentences about your EiA3 venture

QM Intermediate: tutorial week (weeks 45 and 48)

The tutorial is on the day after the lecture, so you cannot wait until the end of the week with your attempts. The tutorial covers the three lectures before it. Each task names the lecture it uses. Start with the tasks on the two earlier lectures.

Tutorial week

  • Monday or Tuesday (75 min): preparation for Wednesday’s lecture (30) · attempt the tutorial tasks that use the earlier lectures (45), AI allowed following the protocol below, mark where you got stuck
  • Wednesday: QM lecture · evening, 30 min: read the lecture material (15) · attempt the tutorial tasks that use today’s lecture (15)
  • Thursday: tutorial (exam preparation session), bring your attempts and the points where you got stuck
  • Friday or weekend (about 110 min): redo the tasks that you got wrong in the tutorial, on your own (45) · the recall questions and practice tasks of Wednesday’s lecture (65) · “Make it yours”

Working with AI, in one paragraph

Use Tilly and other AI tools with the protocol Attempt → Ask → Verify → Explain. Attempt the task yourself first and find where you are stuck. Ask for a hint or for a question that helps you find your mistake, not for a full solution. Verify every answer against the data, the course notation, and the R output. Then explain the result in your own words. The exam tests what you can do with the output that the exam gives you. With this protocol, AI improves this skill instead of replacing it.

If you fall behind

Do not try to catch up by reading everything you missed. Go to the chapter of the most recent lecture. Read its lecture material once, answer its recall questions, and do its first practice task. Bring what you cannot solve to the next tutorial or to Tilly. One finished round on the current lecture is worth more than reading three older chapters without practising. The Mathematics Refresher and R Refresher are there so that something you missed in an earlier course never stops you in this one.
