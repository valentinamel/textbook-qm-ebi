How to succeed in this course

Success in QM is not about talent for mathematics. It is about running a short, repeatable weekly cycle: prepare before class, work actively in class, practise after class, and bring your attempts to the tutorial. Every lecture chapter is built around this cycle, and every part of it carries a stated time budget so you always know what “enough” looks like.

Why this rhythm works: preparation means the lecture is your second pass over each idea, not your first, so class time can go into worked examples and questions instead of definitions. Same-day retrieval interrupts forgetting when it is cheapest to fix. Spaced review items in each chapter keep earlier lectures warm, because the exam is cumulative. And the tutorials only work as exam preparation if you arrive with attempts — including wrong ones, which are the most useful kind.

The total ask outside class: about 3.5 hours per week for QM Basic in two-lecture weeks (less in tutorial weeks), and about 2 hours per week for QM Intermediate. That is the whole plan. Students who spread these hours as scheduled below consistently outperform students who batch them before the exam.

Printable weekly checklists

Four week types cover the whole block. Print this page (the checklists print cleanly without the site navigation) or copy the relevant list into your planner at the start of each week.

QM Basic — week with two lectures (weeks 43, 45, 47)

Two-lecture week — about 3.5 hours outside class

  • Friday before (30 min, free morning): prep videos for Monday’s lecture, prep task, prediction
  • Monday: 14:45 lecture · evening, 15 min retrieval
  • Tuesday (60 min, free day): practice for Monday’s lecture (worked, faded, unassisted) · flagged review items from earlier lectures
  • Wednesday (45 min, around the single EiA slot): prep videos for Thursday’s lecture, prep task, prediction
  • Thursday: 10:45 lecture · afternoon, 15 min retrieval
  • Friday (60 min): practice for Thursday’s lecture · utility prompt, two sentences · start next week’s loop (prep for Monday)

QM Basic — week with one lecture and a tutorial (weeks 44, 46, 48, 49)

Lecture-plus-tutorial week

  • Friday before (30 min): prep for Monday’s lecture
  • Monday: 14:45 lecture · 15 min retrieval
  • Tuesday (60 min): practice for Monday’s lecture
  • Wednesday (60 min): attempt the tutorial tasks yourself first, AI allowed under the protocol, mark stuck points
  • Thursday: 10:45 tutorial (exam prep session), bring attempts · 15 min: redo the missed task
  • Friday (60 min): finish the tutorial set unassisted · utility prompt · prep for Monday

QM Intermediate — standard week (one Wednesday lecture)

The year 2 week is denser (two or three other lectures plus EiA3 and their tutorials), so the QM cycle is one loop, spread wide.

Standard week — about 2 hours outside class

  • Monday or Tuesday (30 min, before the lecture): prep video, prep question, R warm-up, bring the output
  • Wednesday: QM lecture (check the time, it changes by week) · evening, 15 min retrieval
  • Thursday or Friday (60–75 min, around your EiA3 slot): after-class practice — faded task, unassisted set, the full-pipeline item where the set has one · one review item from an earlier lecture · utility prompt, two sentences about your EiA3 venture

QM Intermediate — tutorial week (weeks 45 and 48)

The tutorial is the day after the lecture, so attempts cannot wait for the end of the week. The tutorial covers the three preceding lectures, and only its last part depends on Wednesday’s lecture — the task sheets are ordered to match, and that ordering is deliberate.

Tutorial week

  • Monday or Tuesday (75 min): prep for Wednesday’s lecture (30) · attempt the tutorial tasks that rest on the earlier lectures (45), AI allowed under the protocol, mark stuck points
  • Wednesday: QM lecture · evening, 30 min: attempt the remaining tutorial tasks that use today’s material
  • Thursday: tutorial (exam prep session), bring attempts and stuck points
  • Friday (45 min): redo what the tutorial corrected, unassisted · utility prompt

Working with AI, in one paragraph

Use Tilly and other AI tools under the standing protocol Attempt → Ask → Verify → Explain: attempt the task yourself first and locate exactly where you are stuck; ask for a hint or a diagnostic question rather than a full solution; verify everything against the data, the course notation, and R output; then explain the result in your own words. The exam tests what you can do with supplied output; the protocol makes AI sharpen that ability instead of replacing it.

If you fall behind

Do not try to catch up by reading. Go to the most recent lecture’s chapter, do its retrieval set, then its faded task, and bring what breaks to the next tutorial or to Tilly. One completed cycle on the current lecture is worth more than three passive re-reads of older ones. The Mathematics Refresher and R Refresher exist precisely so that a gap from an earlier course never blocks this one.
