External Video Guide

The videos are an extra tool. They explain the key ideas of the course in another way, and they can use a different notation. Where a video and the lecture material differ, the lecture material and its notation come first.

This page lists the external videos that the course uses. Every clip has one of three purposes:

  • Required before class: watch it before the lecture. These clips are also listed in that lecture’s Video preparation box.
  • Review when needed: the clip explains a concept from the lecture material. Use it after the lecture, after an absence, or before practice if you do not yet understand the concept well.
  • Optional extension: the clip adds another example or more depth. It is not required.

Only clips labelled Required before class are compulsory preparation. Review and extension clips add no exam material. If a video differs from this textbook in scope or notation, follow the textbook. QM Basic mainly uses MIT OpenCourseWare pages, which include videos and transcripts. QM Intermediate uses Stanford’s Statistical Learning series. The complete collections are the MIT probability playlist and the Stanford Statistical Learning playlist.

Supplementary probability reading

MIT OpenCourseWare offers Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis’s Introduction to Probability: Selected Textbook Summary Material. The official page includes the PDF download. This 77-page text is a reference, not required reading. Read only a section that a lecture asks you to read, or that you need for review.

Basic Lecture 1: Introduction to data analysis

No external video is assigned. The lecture chapter lists the preparation.

Basic Lecture 2: Probability foundations and random variables

Required before class

  • 01.2 Sample Space: outcomes, sample spaces, and events.
  • 01.4 Probability Axioms: non-negativity, normalisation, and additivity.
  • 05.2 Definition of Random Variables: a random variable as a function from outcomes to numbers.

Review when needed

  • 01.3 Sample Space Examples: more examples of outcomes and events.
  • 01.5 Simple Properties of Probabilities: complement and addition rules derived from the axioms.
  • 01.10 Interpretations and Uses of Probabilities: how the meaning of a probability depends on the model.

Optional extension

  • 01.7 A Discrete Example: a worked discrete probability model.

Basic Lecture 3: Discrete distributions, expectation, and variance

Required before class

  • 05.3 Probability Mass Functions: point probabilities and the normalisation rule.
  • 05.8 Expectation: expectation as a probability-weighted average.

Review when needed

  • 05.4 Bernoulli and Indicator Random Variables: coding an event as 1 and its complement as 0.
  • 06.2 Variance: expected squared distance from the mean. This lecture teaches variance and the standard deviation.

Basic Lecture 4: Conditioning, Bayes’ rule, and independence

Required before class

  • 02.2 Conditional Probabilities: limiting attention to the outcomes in the condition and rescaling their probabilities.
  • 02.8 Bayes’ Rule: reversing a condition using base rates and likelihoods.

Review when needed

  • 02.6 The Multiplication Rule: writing a joint probability with a conditional probability.
  • 02.7 Total Probability Theorem: combining conditional probabilities across a partition (groups that do not overlap and together cover all outcomes).
  • 03.3 Independence of Two Events: the conditional and product definitions of event independence, which this lecture covers.

Optional extension

  • 02.3 A Die Roll Example: a worked conditioning example.

Basic Lecture 5: Joint random variables, covariance, and correlation

Required before class

  • 06.7 Joint PMFs and the Expected Value Rule: joint probabilities and expectations involving two variables.
  • 12.5 Covariance: covariance as an expected product of deviations.
  • 12.8 The Correlation Coefficient: standardising covariance.

Review when needed

  • 07.2 Conditional PMFs: the distribution of one variable at a fixed value of another variable.
  • 07.4 Independence of Random Variables: checking whether every joint probability equals the product of the two corresponding marginal probabilities.
  • 12.10 Interpreting the Correlation Coefficient: what correlation does and does not describe.

Optional extension

  • 12.6 Covariance Properties: additional algebraic properties of covariance.

Basic Lecture 6: Continuous distributions and the normal model

Required before class

  • 08.2 Probability Density Functions: density, area, and interval probability.
  • 08.3 Uniform and Piecewise Constant PDFs: constant density over an interval.
  • 08.8 Normal Random Variables: normal shape and parameters.

Review when needed

  • 08.9 Calculation of Normal Probabilities: standardisation and normal interval probabilities.
  • 08.7 Cumulative Distribution Functions: cumulative probability for discrete and continuous variables.

Basic Lecture 7: Samples, parameters, and estimators

The MIT clips for Lectures 7 to 9 write \(M_n\) for the sample mean, which this book writes \(\bar X\), and \(\hat\Theta\) for an estimator.

Required before class

  • 20.3 The Sample Mean and Some Terminology: parameters, estimators, estimates, and the sample mean.

Basic Lecture 8: Sampling distributions and the central limit theorem

Required before class

  • 19.2 The Central Limit Theorem: the normal approximation for standardised sums and means.

Basic Lecture 9: The t distribution and confidence intervals

Required before class

  • 20.5 Confidence Intervals: what “95% confidence” means over repeated samples.
  • 20.7 Confidence Intervals for the Mean When the Variance Is Unknown: replacing \(\sigma\) by the sample standard deviation and using a t distribution. This lecture starts with the t distribution.
  • Introduction to Hypothesis Testing: the null model, a two-sided alternative, and the p-value.

Review when needed

  • 20.6 Confidence Intervals for the Estimation of the Mean: the interval for the mean when the variance is known. It is background for the interval with unknown variance that the lecture uses.

Basic Lecture 10: Testing decisions, errors, power, and a first look at regression

Required before class

  • Introduction to Hypothesis Testing: review the null model, two-sided alternatives, and p-values. One-sided alternatives are optional reading in this lecture and are not part of the exam.
  • Examples Identifying Type I and Type II Errors: decision errors in context.
